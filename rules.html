How it is done aboard the ship.

Oh, you would like to know how it is played. Of course. Here is all of it, from the ideas underneath to the dice on the table. If you only want one word explained, the Ledger is quicker.

◆ NATIVE RULES, EXPERIMENTAL These are the Cantica rules as currently drafted for the Foundry system. They may change.

Contents
    Part I

    The ideas underneath

    Cantica is a game about ordinary people aboard a generational ark ship, held together by paperwork, song and habit. The rules are small on purpose, and they are all built from a few ideas.

    The players always roll

    The GM never rolls dice. People and powers on the other side of a roll act through a Rating, which is simply how many successes you need.

    The target never moves

    A white die succeeds on a 7 or better, always. A harder task asks for more successes; it never changes the number you are trying to roll.

    Colour is risk

    Only two special dice exist. The amber Margin is always rolled, and may twist or bless the roll. Violet dice are the Bound: easier to succeed with, but dangerous. Everything else is white.

    Your flaws are worth something

    When a Hindrance bites, you earn a Stamp. Trouble is a resource, and the characters people remember are the ones with a habit they cannot help.

    The ship pushes back

    Every Margin of Error gives the GM Scrutiny, kept where the whole table can see it. Escalation is earned, not arbitrary: the ship is pushing back, the GM is not picking on anyone.

    Seniority is the ship's wealth

    You grow by answering three questions at Compline, not by killing things. Tenure buys skills and Traits, and the Tenure you have earned sets your Grade.

    Small numbers

    Skills run 0 to 2. Traits are rank 1 or 2. A typical pool is 3 to 5 dice and the most you can ever roll is 7, so every die counts and every Stamp matters.

    A roll, at the table

    1. The GM describes the moment and says how hard it is: a Difficulty from D0 to D3, or the Rating of whoever is in the way.
    2. You choose how you try: the skill that matches your approach, and the one Trait that fits best.
    3. You roll the pool: the Margin, plus a white die for each reason you have.
    4. Count your successes against the Difficulty: Denied, With Conditions, Approved or Commended. Watch the Margin for an Error or a Grace.
    5. Afterwards, you may spend Stamps for another die, and the GM may spend Scrutiny to complicate the roll, which a Stamp can cancel.

    What you need

    A handful of ten-sided dice (white, one amber, and a few violet, if you have them), a character, and a table of people who are happy to be a little unfair to each other in the name of a good story. In the Foundry system, the dice, the pool and all of the Stamps and Scrutiny are handled for you on the roll's chat card.
